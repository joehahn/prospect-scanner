# Follow-up test arm: one proof, one easy next step

**Version 2, 2026-11-17.**

*v2: no write-up, and a proof only when it is close to their work. Two of the
first proof_step drafts, to a pharma operations lead and a state agency's
applications director, closed by asking whether "a short write-up" would be
useful, and the operator objected both times: an offer to send a resource is
a tactic, which his rule for this message bars. The agency director's draft
also carried a study of which model a build needs, a tangent for someone
running AI adoption under governance; the operator cut it as a distraction
that made the note longer than it needed to be. The question is now a plain
one about their work, and a proof that is not close is left out.*

*v1: the operator approved an alternating test of the message after a silent
accept. Half of those messages are drafted as before (draft-cold-note, "The
message after an accepted connection": an open door, no tactic). This half
replaces the open door with one proof and one easy next step. Both outside
reviewers, from two different makers, found that every opportunity on record
came from a note that stated an offer, and that the people who did reply were
sent proof (case-study links) only after asking for it. About 0 of the
open-door follow-ups had drawn a reply. The test decides; neither arm is
assumed better. The drafter's own instructions are frozen and are not edited
for this.*

THIS MESSAGE IS IN A TEST ARM. For this message only, the following replaces
two rules in "The message after an accepted connection": "An open door, and
nothing to do" and "No tactic". Everything else in that section still holds:
why he asked to connect, the offer for the first time, sized to their seat,
and one plain note in his voice.

- **One proof, with its link, only if it is close to their work.** Choose the
  one item from CITABLE PROOF POINTS that answers something their seat owns,
  and say in a few plain words what it shows that they would care about. Give
  its URL. Only one; never a list. Close means they would act on what it shows
  (a study of which model a build needs is close for someone who pays for or
  chooses models, not for someone running adoption or governance). If none is
  that close, use none, and say so in the NOTES; most notes will carry none.
- **One easy next step, as a question about their work.** One short question
  they can answer in a few words without a meeting, about whether there is
  work the offer fits: "Is there an AI build on your teams' list for next year
  where an extra senior hand would help?" Never an offer to send something (a
  write-up, a checklist, a case study), and never a call, a meeting, a demo or
  a referral; those wait until they have replied.
- Keep the whole note short: three to six sentences.

In the NOTES, say "TEST ARM: proof_step", which proof you chose and why.
