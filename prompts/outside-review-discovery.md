# Outside review of discovery only

**Version 1, 2026-11-19.**

*v1: the operator asked for a review of discovery alone between the quarterly
whole-system reviews, after a day's reading showed it skewed: conference
agendas fill nearly every target, the agenda finder leaned on events that had
already answered and starved newer targets, and 587 people judged in 30 days
fitted no target. Two changes adopted from the last outside review (a silence
penalty in the judge and a hard firm cool-off) were reversed the same day, so
every suggestion here is tested against the operator's numbers before it goes
in: a change is adopted only if it removes something, or if a cheap test or the
outcome numbers show a gain.*

---

You are reviewing **only the discovery half** of a prospect-discovery system
that one independent AI consultant (the operator) uses to find clients: how it
finds firms and people, which sources and searches it runs, how it files them
under the operator's targets, and how the screen and judge rate them. Drafting
and sending are out of scope. It was built by a different AI model, which may be
anchored on its own choices; you are asked for an independent, critical read,
not agreement.

The operator sends every message by hand and nothing touches LinkedIn
automatically. That is fixed, not something to review.

**The goal:** many more high-quality prospects a day, spread across the
operator's targets in proportion to how well each converts. A high-quality
prospect can buy, has a dated reason to buy now, and fits an offer. The
operator's bar is five new people rated 4 or 5 a day.

The packet holds the docs, the discovery prompts, the operator's config
(targets, events, discovery sources, search lists), the header comment of each
source file, and the numbers: people judged and rated per target and per
source, per industry, accepts per target, the state of the weekly searches,
and the agenda finder's record.

Answer these, citing the packet's evidence (a file, a section, a number):

1. **Bias.** Which targets are over- or under-fed relative to how they convert,
   and which mechanism causes it? Is any target fed mainly by one source, so
   that the target's numbers are really that source's?
2. **Waste.** Which sources, searches or stages spend money or judging on
   people who fit no target or rarely rate well? What would you cut, and what
   would be lost?
3. **Missing sources.** Which data sources, signals or channels would find the
   operator's targets that the system does not use? Be specific: named
   databases, filings, registries, procurement portals, grant and award lists,
   event series, associations and their member directories, job boards,
   communities, partner and vendor ecosystems, podcasts, regulators'
   publications. For each: which target it serves, what it would find, how it
   is reached without automating LinkedIn and within robots.txt, and roughly
   what it costs.
4. **Targets.** Should any target be split, merged, added or dropped, judged by
   the yield and accept numbers? Name the evidence.
5. **Measurement.** Can the operator tell from these numbers which source is
   working? What is missing from how discovery is measured?

Write a report of at most about 2,500 words, in this form:

## Top findings
Up to 8, most important first. For each: what is wrong, the evidence, the change,
how to test it cheaply before adopting it, and the effect you expect.

## Missing data sources
Most promising first: what it is, the target it serves, what it would find, how
it is reached, its cost.

## Cut list
Sources, searches or stages to stop, each with one line of why and what is lost.

## Where you are unsure
What the packet could not tell you, and what data would settle it.

Be concrete and blunt. Do not restate the packet. Do not praise.
